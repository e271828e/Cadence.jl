1884	### D-066 — Two ways to apply one plan: specialized `apply!` vs dynamic entry-list walk
1885	
1886	**Status.** ratified
1887	
1888	**Position.** Two ways to apply one compiled plan: specialized
1889	`apply!` (`Getter{P}` lenses, unrolled baked stores, zero-alloc; [§9.5][s9-5]-style
1890	shape check via tree type + literal `===` sweep; ~10–50 ms codegen once per
1891	shape) for iterating services; dynamic entry-list walk (microseconds, no
1892	per-shape codegen, allocation fine per [§7.5][s7-5]) for one-shot init; compiled
1893	readers as the gather twin (cost reads, linearization gather, `capture`) — one
1894	primitive family in the `Build`'s client kit.
1895	
1896	**Spec.** [§7.5][s7-5], [§9.5][s9-5]
1897	
1898	**Rationale.** Recorded only through the rejections below.
1899	
1900	Annotation (2026-10-02): the codegen cost grows with the shape's writes. The
1901	Position's 10 to 50 ms holds up to 32 writes. It measures 0.09 s at 64 writes
1902	and 0.2 s at 128 (`docs/reports/20261002_tuple_walks/`).
1903	
1904	**Rejected.**
1905	- *A single always-specialized form:* per-shape codegen tax on scripted
1906	  one-shot conditions.
1907	- *A single always-dynamic form:* forfeits the zero-alloc trim loop.
1908	- *Per-write convert decisions:* the converter is a resolution-time fact;
1909	  [§9.5][s9-5]'s no-convert-on-write stands for table cells.
